# Supplements: What the Evidence Says

Supplements are an optional extra on top of training, food, sleep and recovery. Most people get far more benefit from getting those right first.

## Creatine monohydrate (strong evidence)
The most researched performance supplement. It increases strength, power output and lean mass gains from resistance training. Standard dose is 3-5 g per day, taken any time of day, every day. A loading phase (about 20 g per day split in 4 doses for 5-7 days) saturates muscle faster but is not required. Expect 1-2 kg of water weight gain early on. Creatine is considered safe for healthy adults at these doses. People with kidney disease or on medication should check with a doctor first. Choose plain creatine monohydrate; other forms have no proven advantage.

## Whey protein (strong evidence as a convenience)
Whey is a high-quality, fast-digesting protein. It does not build more muscle than the same amount of protein from food; its value is convenience. A serving of about 20-40 g fits most people. Whey isolate contains very little lactose, and plant blends (pea plus rice) work well for those avoiding dairy. Count it toward total daily protein.

## Caffeine (strong evidence)
Improves endurance, strength performance and perceived effort. Effective doses are about 3-6 mg per kg of body weight taken 30-60 minutes before training. Up to about 400 mg per day is generally regarded as safe for healthy adults. Stop caffeine at least 6-8 hours before bed, since poor sleep costs more than caffeine gives. Start with a low dose to check tolerance. Avoid or limit with anxiety, high blood pressure, heart rhythm problems or pregnancy, and ask a doctor.

## Vitamin D (situational)
Helpful mainly if blood levels are low, which is common with little sun exposure. A blood test is the best way to know. Typical maintenance supplements are 600-2000 IU per day, but higher doses should be doctor-guided.

## Omega-3 fish oil (limited evidence for performance)
Useful for people who rarely eat oily fish, and has general health benefits. Little direct effect on muscle gain. About 1-2 g combined EPA and DHA per day is common.

## Weak or unnecessary
- BCAAs: not needed if total daily protein is adequate.
- Fat burners: small effects at best, often stimulant-heavy with side effects.
- Testosterone boosters: little credible evidence of benefit in healthy men.
- Pre-workouts: mostly caffeine plus small amounts of citrulline or beta-alanine; check total caffeine.

## Quality and safety
Supplements are lightly regulated, and contamination or mislabelling occurs. Prefer products with third-party testing (for example Informed Sport or NSF Certified for Sport). Do not combine multiple stimulant products. Anyone tested in competitive sport must check the banned-substance rules. Steroids and other performance-enhancing drugs carry serious health risks and require a doctor's supervision; do not provide dosing.
