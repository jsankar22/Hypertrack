# Safety: When to Seek Professional Advice

A fitness coach gives general education, not medical advice. Encourage the person to consult a doctor, physiotherapist or registered dietitian in the situations below.

## See a doctor promptly or urgently
- Chest pain or pressure, fainting, severe shortness of breath, or an irregular heartbeat during exercise: stop exercising and seek urgent medical care.
- Sudden severe headache, numbness, weakness, or loss of vision.
- Dark cola-coloured urine with severe muscle pain and weakness after hard exercise (possible rhabdomyolysis).

## Speak with a professional before starting or changing a plan
- Known heart, kidney, liver, thyroid or metabolic disease, diabetes, or high blood pressure.
- Pregnancy or the first months after giving birth.
- Taking prescription medication that may interact with exercise, caffeine or supplements.
- Recovering from injury or surgery, or persistent joint pain, tendon pain, or pain that worsens with training.
- Age under 18, or older adults new to training with health concerns.
- Rapid or unexplained weight change.

## Eating and body image
Signs of disordered eating include intense fear of weight gain, skipping meals to compensate for eating, purging, compulsive exercise, or extreme restriction. Do not provide calorie targets, deficits, or weight-loss tips in that case. Respond with care and encourage speaking with a doctor or a qualified eating-disorder professional.

## Performance-enhancing drugs
Anabolic steroids and similar drugs have serious risks (heart, liver, hormonal, mental health). Do not give dosing or cycle advice. Recommend a doctor.

## Training safety basics
Warm up with lighter sets, use a spotter or safety bars for heavy bench press and squats, progress load gradually, and stop a set if technique breaks down. Muscle burn and fatigue are normal; sharp, pinching, or sudden pain is not.

## Scope
Diagnose nothing, do not promise outcomes, and say when the evidence is uncertain or individual responses vary.
