# Calculating Calorie and Macro Targets

## Step 1: resting energy (BMR)
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is a widely used estimate of basal metabolic rate (BMR) in kcal per day. Use weight in kg, height in cm and age in years.
- Men: BMR = 10 x weight + 6.25 x height - 5 x age + 5
- Women: BMR = 10 x weight + 6.25 x height - 5 x age - 161

## Step 2: maintenance calories (TDEE)
Multiply BMR by an activity factor:
- Sedentary (desk job, little exercise): 1.2
- Light (exercise 1-3 days per week): 1.375
- Moderate (exercise 3-5 days per week): 1.55
- Very active (hard exercise 6-7 days per week): 1.725
- Extra active (very hard training plus a physical job): 1.9
People commonly overestimate their activity level. If unsure, choose the lower option and adjust using real weight trends.

## Step 3: adjust for the goal
- Cut (fat loss): about 20% below maintenance
- Maintain: maintenance
- Bulk (muscle gain): about 10% above maintenance
These are starting points. Track body weight for 2-3 weeks and adjust by 100-200 kcal.

## Step 4: set macros in grams
1. Protein: 1.8-2.2 g per kg body weight (higher during a cut).
2. Fat: about 25% of calories, and never below about 0.6 g per kg.
3. Carbohydrate: the remaining calories, at 4 kcal per gram.
Calories per gram: protein 4, carbohydrate 4, fat 9, alcohol 7.

## Worked example
A 80 kg, 180 cm, 25-year-old man who trains 3-4 days per week (moderate) wants to gain muscle. BMR is about 1805 kcal, maintenance about 2800 kcal, so a bulk target is about 3080 kcal. Protein 160 g (640 kcal), fat about 85 g (765 kcal), carbohydrate about 417 g (1670 kcal).

## Safety notes
Adult equations are not appropriate for people under 18; they should speak with a doctor or registered dietitian. Anyone with a medical condition, who is pregnant, or who has a history of disordered eating should get individual guidance from a qualified professional.
