# Recovery, Sleep, Rest Days and Deloads

## Why recovery matters
Muscle grows and strength improves during recovery, not during the workout itself. Training stress plus insufficient sleep, food or rest leads to stalled progress and higher injury risk.

## Sleep
Most adults need 7-9 hours per night, and hard-training people often feel best toward the upper end. Short sleep reduces strength, endurance, mood and appetite control. Tips: keep a regular sleep and wake time, keep the room dark, cool and quiet, stop caffeine 6-8 hours before bed, limit alcohol, and avoid heavy screen use right before bed. If daytime tiredness continues despite good sleep habits, or there is loud snoring or breathing pauses, see a doctor.

## Rest days
Most people do best with 1-3 rest days per week. A muscle group generally needs about 48-72 hours before being trained hard again. Active recovery (walking, easy cycling, mobility work) is fine on rest days and can help you feel better, but it is not required.

## Deload weeks
A deload is a planned lighter week to shed accumulated fatigue. Common practice is one every 4-8 weeks, or sooner when signs of fatigue appear. Typical approach: keep the same exercises, cut the number of sets by about 40-50%, keep the weights about the same or slightly lighter, and stop sets well short of failure. Then return to normal training.

Signs it is time for a deload:
- Lifts stall or drop for 2 or more weeks
- Joints feel achy and workouts feel much harder than usual
- Low motivation, poor mood, or disturbed sleep
- Resting heart rate is elevated for several days

## Nutrition for recovery
Eat enough total calories and protein (about 1.6-2.2 g per kg per day), include carbohydrates around hard sessions, drink enough water, and eat a variety of fruit and vegetables. Very low calorie dieting makes recovery harder.

## Soreness
Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is normal after new or harder work and fades with regular training. Soreness is not a reliable measure of a good workout. Sharp, sudden, or joint pain is different and should not be trained through.

## Stress and lifestyle
Work, life and emotional stress add to total load. In busy weeks, reduce volume rather than skipping training entirely. Regular training itself improves sleep and stress in most people.
